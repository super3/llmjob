Privacy Policy

Last updated 26 September 2026

LLMJob is a free Pearl (PRL) miner. It needs very little about you to work, and most of what it does send is shown in public on the network board. This policy says exactly what that is, what we deliberately don't collect, and how long anything lasts.

The short version

  • There are no accounts. The miner needs a payout address and nothing else.
  • While it mines, the app reports its hashrate and hardware to us once a minute, and that report is shown on a public page next to your payout address.
  • The same report carries card health (temperature, power, clocks, fan) and a random rig id. We keep those to diagnose miners and don't publish them.
  • If you join the managed-mining waitlist, we keep the email address you give us and use it only to contact you about managed mining.
  • We don't log IP addresses, we don't set cookies, and we never sell data.
  • We never hold your funds or any private key. The pool pays your address directly.

1. What we collect

LLMJob Earn (the miner)

While mining, the app reports once a minute per graphics card: your payout address, a worker name, the pool region, GPU model, hashrate, accepted shares, VRAM used and total, and the app version. This is what the public network board displays.

The same report also carries readings we keep for diagnosing miners and do not show on the board: rejected shares, each card's temperature, power draw and power limit, core and memory clocks and fan speed, the graphics driver version, the operating system (Windows, Linux or macOS, nothing more specific), whether the app or the command-line miner sent it, how long it has been mining and how long since its last share.

Each install also makes a random rig key the first time it runs and keeps it on your machine. The report carries the key's public half, a short rig id derived from it and a signature, so we can tell your rigs apart and know a report really came from that rig. The rig id is stored with the rest of that rig's report, including its payout address, and is not shown on the board. It has nothing to do with your wallet and can't move funds.

The worker name defaults to your computer's hostname (lower-cased and stripped of punctuation), so if your machine is called something like alex-desktop that is what appears on a public page next to your payout address. Change it in the app's settings if you would rather it didn't.

Managed-mining waitlist

If you sign up on the managed mining page we store the email address you enter, the number of GPUs and the note you add if you fill those in, and when you signed up. Signing up again with the same email updates that entry rather than adding another. We use it only to contact you about managed mining.

What the desktop app keeps to itself

Some things never leave your machine, by design:

  • Mining logs, which are shown in the app and written locally. There is no remote logging endpoint.
  • Your settings, including your merge-mining address, which goes only to the pool.

The app also handles no cryptocurrency private keys at all: a payout address is just a string to it. Nothing signs transactions, and there is no wallet or seed phrase anywhere in it.

Website analytics

We use a privacy-focused analytics service that records page views without cookies and without building a profile of you across sites.

2. What is public

The network page shows every rig currently mining with LLMJob, and the information behind it is public by design: payout addresses (shortened on the page, but returned in full by the API behind it), worker names, GPU models, hashrates, shares, VRAM and client versions. Anyone can look up an address to see its rigs.

The health readings and rig id from section 1 are not on the page and not in the API behind it.

Two consequences worth being explicit about. A payout address is a permanent, publicly searchable identifier on its blockchain, so anything shown beside it is linked to it in public and stays linked. And because the worker name defaults to your hostname, the default behaviour publishes your machine's name unless you change it.

Waitlist signups are never public.

3. What we don't collect

Stated as specifics rather than reassurance, because each is verifiable in our source code, which is public:

  • No IP address logging. Our application records no IP address, no geolocation, no User-Agent and no referrer, and has no database column for any of them. The region shown for a miner is the pool region it connects to, not one we infer about you. The honest caveat: any server that answers your browser or your rig — our host, the analytics service, the font CDN, the mining pool — necessarily sees your IP address in order to reply, and keeps its own logs. We mean that we don't store one, not that none exists anywhere.
  • No device telemetry beyond what section 1 lists. The app collects no operating system username, file paths, MAC address, installed-software inventory or crash-analytics stream, and ships no analytics SDK.
  • No cookies of our own, and no cross-site tracking or advertising pixels.
  • No accounts, so no passwords and no profile.
  • No sale of personal information, and no sharing of it for advertising.

6. Security

Traffic is encrypted in transit. The app never handles a wallet key, so there is none for it to leak, and payouts go from the pool straight to your address. The only key it holds is the rig key from section 1, which can sign "this is rig X" and nothing else.

No system is perfectly secure, and we can't promise otherwise. If you find a vulnerability, please tell us privately at help@llmjob.com before disclosing it.

7. Your choices and rights

Some controls are immediate and in your hands:

  • Change the worker name in the app before you start, so your hostname isn't published.
  • Stop mining to stop reporting to the public board; the row ages off by itself.
  • Run the command-line miner with --no-report to mine without sending any report at all.
  • Leave the waitlist form's optional fields blank; only the email is required.

Depending on where you live you may also have rights to access, correct, export, restrict or delete personal data, and to object to processing or complain to a regulator. Ask us at help@llmjob.com — for example to be removed from the waitlist — and we will respond within the time your law requires.

8. Children

LLMJob is not intended for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect their personal data. If you believe a child has given us information, contact us and we will delete it.

9. International transfers

Our providers operate internationally, so data may be processed outside the country you live in.

10. Changes

We will update this policy as LLMJob changes, and the date at the top will change with it. For material changes we will make a reasonable effort to give notice in the product or the Discord before they take effect.
